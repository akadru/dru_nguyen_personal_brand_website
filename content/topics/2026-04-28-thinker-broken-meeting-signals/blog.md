> **Most operators added AI to their stack and stopped there.** They never let it touch the most expensive recurring asset they have: the team meeting.

---

## The cold open

I started counting last quarter. Across Xood, Skylight, and three advisory rooms, I sat in 41 team meetings in eight weeks. I noticed something specific. The teams that had adopted AI for everything else, prospect research, drafting, scorecards, were running their meetings exactly the way they did in 2024.

The AI was doing the work *around* the meeting. The meeting itself was untouched.

That gap is where the leverage hides. Three signals tell me a team is leaving it on the table.

---

## Signal 1. The "circle back with the numbers" reflex

A question gets asked in the meeting. Nobody has the answer. Someone says, *"let me circle back with the numbers."*

The first time, that's normal. The fifth time in a quarter, it's a system failure.

I watched a Skylight ops team pull the same question into Slack three weeks running. Same number, same person promising to find it, same delay. Their AI stack was excellent. Their pre-meeting prep was zero.

**What this signal means:** Your team is using AI for production tasks (drafting, summarizing, scraping) and skipping the upstream task it's actually best at, *retrieving the right data into the room before the meeting starts.*

**What to do:** Five minutes before any meeting, run one prompt: *"Based on [last month's scorecard, the 3 open issues, the budget memo I'm pasting], surface the 5 questions someone in this meeting is most likely to ask. Show the answer next to each."* You'll show up with the questions answered and the conversation moves.

---

## Signal 2. The same argument repeats week over week

You debate the rebrand timing in week 1. You debate it again in week 4. You'll debate it in week 8.

In a properly tooled team, an argument resolves once and the resolution becomes a referenceable artifact. In an under-tooled team, the resolution lives in someone's head, and that head changes its mind by the next meeting.

I had this exact failure at Xood last fall. We re-litigated the same hiring decision four times because nobody owned the *artifact* of the decision. The meeting kept happening. The decision kept un-happening.

**What this signal means:** AI has perfect recall, and your team isn't using it. You're paying recurring meeting time to retrieve memory that should live in a doc.

**What to do:** End every meeting with a 90-second prompt: *"Based on this transcript, write the 3 decisions made, the 3 owners, the 3 deadlines. Format as a single paragraph."* Drop it in a shared channel. Reference it the next time the topic surfaces. The argument resolves once and stays resolved.

---

## Signal 3. The pre-meeting Slack scramble

Nine minutes before the call, your team is scrambling. *"Anyone have the latest deck?" "Who pulled the numbers?" "What's the agenda again?"*

This is the most common signal. I see it in 80% of the teams I sit in. It's also the most fixable.

The pre-meeting scramble is a symptom of one thing: nobody used AI to produce the brief *before* anyone joined the call.

**What this signal means:** Your team treats AI as a personal productivity tool, not a meeting infrastructure tool. Each individual uses Claude or ChatGPT to draft their own emails, but no one is responsible for using it to brief the room.

**What to do:** Assign one person per recurring meeting to own the pre-brief. Their job is 30 minutes of AI prep before the call. Walking in with a deck or a 1-pager. Anthropic's [Claude Design](https://www.anthropic.com/news), launched April 17, is purpose-built for this. The brief becomes the meeting. The scramble disappears.

---

## What these signals actually cost

Pull a calendar from one of your top managers. Count the team meetings. Multiply by an honest estimate of meeting length. Multiply again by the number of attendees.

For me, across two businesses and the advisory work, that number is 22 hours a week of senior-team time spent in meetings. If even 30% of that time is wasted by these three signals, that's seven hours of senior leverage per week, evaporating into "circle backs" and re-litigated decisions.

Seven hours is a Saturday and a half. Most operators I know would pay good money for a Saturday and a half. They are paying for it. They just don't see the bill.

---

## The bigger pattern

The three signals are different shapes of the same root cause. **Your team has AI in the production layer. They don't have it in the coordination layer.** The production layer is where individuals work. The coordination layer is where the team works together.

Coordination is the messier layer to fix. Individual productivity tools have a clear owner: you. Meeting prep has a fuzzy owner: it's the leader's job by default, but in practice nobody owns it.

That fuzziness is the gap. It's also the leverage.

---

## The question worth sitting with

If your team adopted AI for everything except meetings, you've solved the easy half of the problem. The hard half is coordination.

**Whose job is it on your team to make sure no meeting starts without an AI-prepared artifact in the room?** If the answer is "nobody," you've found the bottleneck. If the answer is "the senior leader," you've found a Buyback Loop opportunity in the Dan Martell sense, that role is too expensive to keep doing yourself.
