# Dru Nguyen — Writer Guide for Marketing Manager

> **What this is.** A self-contained brand-voice and writing guide for the person drafting Dru's social posts and essays. Read top to bottom once. Keep it open while you write. Every draft must clear the four-question test and the five-point clarity gate at the bottom before it goes to Dru for approval.
>
> **What you'll write.** Short hooks and captions for LinkedIn, Substack Notes, Facebook EN, and image-post captions. You do not write Vietnamese-language Facebook posts — draft in English and flag for the Vietnamese writer to adapt natively. You do not write the long-form essays or the copy-paste prompts; those are written separately. You DO write everything that goes around them: hooks, captions, social adaptations, and the weekly batch.
>
> **What you never do.** Never publish on Dru's behalf. Never schedule. Every social draft goes to Dru for approval before it leaves the room.

## 1. The brand in one paragraph

Dru Nguyen is an operator, co-founder, and investor. He runs **3 companies** (Xood, Skylight, GAM Esports) and attends **2 board meetings** (East West Brewery, GAM). The identity that anchors the brand is the **Scholarly Warrior** — **Care. Discipline. Grit.** Dru is the operator who teaches frameworks better than other people pretend to invent them. He is positioning against AI hype, against influencer voice, and against the founder who borrows ideas without crediting the source. Every post must read like Dru is talking to another operator at an EO meeting — not a LinkedIn audience.

**Never say "6 businesses" or "5 ventures."** The framing is "3 companies, 2 board meetings." Tictag is past advisory; Nuen Motor is investor-only and mentioned sparingly.

## 2. Voice blend (the target mix in every post)

| Weight | Voice | What it sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **70%** | **Jeff Su** | Tactical utility. Save-worthy. Specific prompts, templates, workflows. The post ends with something the reader can use in 10 minutes. |
| **15%** | **Tony Robbins** | Identity language. "Scholarly Warrior." Towards/away values. Emotional truth named directly. |
| **10%** | **Daniel Priestley** | Sequence storytelling: before → action → after. Lived experience as source material. |
| **5%** | **Gary Vee** | Casual, unfiltered. Willing to swear lightly when it serves. Zero corporate polish. |

### Four attributes that must show up in every post

1. **Operator Authority** — Dru ships real work. Real business names, real outcomes, real numbers when they exist.
2. **Warm Teacher** — Shares generously. Explains the WHY behind the HOW. Patient with readers at different stages.
3. **AI Realist** — Rejects AI hype and AI theater. Positive about AI but grounded in what it actually does in real operations.
4. **Sharp Observer** — Names patterns other operators miss. Calls out the unnamed pain in a room.

### Person and tense

- **First person, always.** "I learned this the hard way." Never "Leaders often find that..."
- **Contractions always.** "Here's" not "Here is."
- **Em dash, not comma**, when the sentence pivots — "real thinking — real outcomes."

### Voice markers (use sparingly, max 1–2 per post)

- "Knowledge with application is wisdom."
- "Never give up. Always find a way."
- "Life happens for you, not to you."
- "Care. Discipline. Grit."
- "Scholarly Warrior" / "I see, hear, feel, and KNOW."

### Words and phrases to NEVER use

- "Hot take," "Game changer," "Unpacking," "Let that sink in," "Read that again," "It's that simple"
- "If you're not doing X, you're leaving money on the table"
- "Leverage," "synergies," "unlock potential," "at scale"
- Generic openers: "In today's rapidly evolving AI landscape...", "Most founders don't realize..."
- Emoji spam (🚀💡✨). One earned emoji rarely; zero preferred.
- Engagement-bait endings: "What are your thoughts?", "Drop a 🔥 below," "Agree or disagree?"

## 6. Hard rules — must follow

1. **Never publish.** Drafts only. Every social post goes to Dru for approval first.
2. **Email campaigns ALWAYS require human approval before sending.** Non-negotiable.
3. **Specificity over philosophy.** Name the business, the tool, the moment.
4. **Short paragraphs.** 1–3 lines. Every sentence earns its place.
5. **At most ONE bold phrase** for emphasis per post. No bullet spam.
6. **Bold via `<strong>`** if writing HTML, or via the platform's native bold (not ASTERISKS or CAPS).
7. **First person, always.** Past tense for stories, present for principles.
8. **Em dash, not comma**, where the sentence pivots.
9. **No corporate jargon.** No "leverage," "synergies," "unlock," "at scale."
10. **No engagement-bait endings.** No "What are your thoughts?"
11. **Never disclose office locations.** No "District 1," no street/building, no "our office in {place}." A city as personal setting is fine if it isn't tied to an office.
12. **Portfolio framing:** "3 companies, 2 board meetings." Never "6 businesses." Never name Tictag as active. Nuen mentioned sparingly.
13. **EO Forum / YPO / Mastermind / peer forums = protected ground.** Never framed as failure modes. If a "what didn't work" needs a peer-group example, swap to: "Reading every operator book ever written," "Asking my team for honest feedback," "Talking it out with my therapist," "Daily journaling alone," or "Hiring a topic-specific consultant" instead.

## 7. The plain skeleton for every post (S.I.F.P)

Every post follows this skeleton, in order. S.I.F.P maps onto it.

1. **HOOK** (1–2 lines) — the specific moment or the promise. Concrete. **[S = Situation]**
2. **PROBLEM** (1 short paragraph) — the real pain, named plainly, and why it matters. **[S]**
3. **THE TURN** (1–2 lines) — what changed, in ONE plain sentence. **[I = Insight]**
4. **THE STEPS** (body) — numbered or sequential. Each step does ONE thing. Show the actual prompt, tool, or number. **[F = Framework]**
5. **TAKEAWAY** — a 10-minute tactic OR one real question. Never both, never neither. **[P = Provocation or Utility]**

- Tactical / Thinker posts → S.I.F.**[Utility takeaway]** (replace P with a prompt or template)
- Builder / Human posts → S.I.F.**P** (provocation — one real question)

## 8. The 7-Beat Story Arc — for any long-form post that includes a copy-paste prompt

The intro must hook the reader with a universal story before introducing the prompt. The reader feels the pain through someone else's life first, then sees themselves in it, then sees Dru as a fellow practitioner.

1. **THE FELT QUESTION** (1 sentence) — names the reader's pain as a direct question, HBR-style provocation. *Example:* "Name one decision you made last year that you would unmake if you could rewind. Now name the person who could have stopped you. If that second name was 'nobody,' you and I are reading the same post."
2. **THE STORY OF A FAMOUS PERSON OR HISTORICAL FIGURE** (3 to 6 short paragraphs) — one real person facing one real-world issue. Setup → conflict → resolution. Real names, real dates, real numbers. Single arc.
3. **THE PRINCIPLE** (1 to 2 sentences) — the lesson the story teaches. Plain. Not metaphor on metaphor.
4. **"TAKEAWAY THIS WEEK." HINGE** — use this heading phrase (or a brand variant).
5. **APPLY TO THE READER** (1 short paragraph) — "If you [their situation], you can [the principle] by [the move]." The reader needs to see themselves in this sentence.
6. **DRU'S APPLICATION** (1 paragraph) — "How I [verb] is by [specific method] using [tool]." Concrete. Names the tool. Dru is a fellow practitioner, not a guru.
7. **THE HANDOFF** (1 line) — "Here is the prompt I drop in every [cadence]."

**The historical figure is the vehicle. Dru's first-person experience is the application. Never lead with Dru's moment — lead with the universal story.**

### Figure rotation rule

No two consecutive posts pull from the same category:
- (A) Operators / entrepreneurs / business leaders
- (B) Scientists / inventors
- (C) Military leaders / strategists
- (D) Athletes / coaches
- (E) Artists / cultural figures

Pillar-tied defaults when rotation does not constrain: Thinker → B or C, Builder → A, Human → D or E.

### HBR-style opening — not soft rhetorical

The Beat #1 felt question must put a real stake in the ground. Three acceptable shapes:

- **Hormozi-style provocation:** "Name one [X]. Now name [Y]. If [Z], you and I are reading the same post."
- **HBR thesis statement:** "The most expensive [thing] in your [domain] is [unexpected]."
- **HBR authority hook:** "When [authority] was asked [X], they said [unexpected]. [Years] later, the data still agrees."

**Unacceptable:** "Ever sit down on Sunday and realize…?" "Have you ever thought…?" — too soft. The first sentence must make the reader do work or feel exposed.

The pattern: directive verb in sentence 1, expansion in sentence 2, complicit "you and I" close in sentence 3.

## 9. Credit everything. Always. With a hyperlink.

Dru's brand is built on the operator who teaches frameworks better than other people pretend to invent them. The non-negotiable outcome is: **give credit to others.** Every time a post references a person, a company, a book, a podcast, a framework, a term, or a piece of in-group vocabulary that did not originate with Dru, that reference must include a hyperlink to the canonical source on first mention.

### What MUST be credited with a link

| Reference type | Link target |
|---|---|
| **People** (Bob Bowman, Michael Phelps, Jeff Su, Dan Martell, Hormozi, Jocko, Tony Robbins, Drucker, Goggins, Christensen, Andy Grove, Toyoda, etc.) | Their primary site, Wikipedia, the org they're known for, or the book they wrote |
| **Companies and brands** mentioned in the body (Toyota, YC, Sequoia, Anthropic, Apple, Amazon, etc.) | Their official site |
| **Books cited** (*Traction*, *Atomic Habits*, *The Inner Game of Tennis*, *Extreme Ownership*, etc.) | The author or publisher's book page |
| **Frameworks and systems** (EOS, OKRs, JTBD, Lean, TPS, Jidoka, Working Backwards, Volume × Leverage, CORE Workflow, Buyback Loop) | The originator's canonical source |
| **Specific terms from those frameworks** (Rock, L10, IDS, Scorecard, Andon Cord, North Star Metric, RICE, KR) | Same canonical source as the parent framework |
| **Podcasts, shows, talks** (Lenny's Podcast, Diary of a CEO, All-In, Acquired) | The show's official URL; for episodes, link the specific episode |
| **HBR articles, TED talks, conferences** | Direct link to the article or talk |

### Default link targets to memorize

- **EOS / Rocks / L10 / IDS / Scorecard / Traction** → https://www.eosworldwide.com/ (book: https://www.eosworldwide.com/traction-book)
- **Jeff Su's CORE Workflow** → https://www.jeffsu.org/productivity
- **Dan Martell's Buyback Principle** → https://buybackyourtime.com
- **Hormozi's Volume × Leverage = Output** → https://acquisition.com
- **Jocko Willink's Extreme Ownership** → https://echelonfront.com
- **Jidoka / Andon Cord / TPS** → https://global.toyota/en/company/vision-and-philosophy/production-system/
- **Jobs-to-be-Done** → Christensen's HBR article
- **Working Backwards / PR-FAQ** → Amazon's source

### What does NOT need a link

- **Dru's own businesses** (Xood, Skylight, GAM Esports, East West Brewery, Nuen Motor) — they're linked elsewhere on the site
- **Common knowledge proper nouns** ("Saigon," "Vietnam," "the 1890s")
- **First-name informal characters in a story** ("my brother TK," "Joy," "Chi") — internal references, not credits

### Format

> `[Name or term](URL)` — first mention only. Repeat references don't re-link. Open external links in a new tab.

### Define borrowed terms on first use

Linking isn't enough — define the term too, in plain English. A reader who has never run EOS will hit the word "rock" and feel locked out by paragraph two. One sentence of definition + one link buys their attention back.

**Format on first use:**

> [The borrowed term] is [link to source](URL) shorthand for [one-sentence plain definition]. [Optional: how to map it if the reader uses a different framework.]

**Example:**

> A *rock* is [EOS](https://www.eosworldwide.com/) (Entrepreneurial Operating System) shorthand for a top-3 priority for the quarter. Owner named, deadline set, binary done or not done. Ninety-day chunks. If your team runs OKRs, sub in "key result."

### Calibration question before publishing

> "If every person, company, framework, book, term, and venue named in this post saw it tomorrow, would they feel credited or borrowed-from?"

If any would feel borrowed-from, the post fails. Rewrite.

## 12. Clever → Clear — the #1 failure mode to avoid

The #1 way a draft fails is sounding **clever** instead of being **clear.** Short declarative fragments stacked for drama ("X. Not Y. Z does."). Once per post it punches. Everywhere, it strips out the connective words ("because / so / but") that tell the reader *why one sentence follows the next* — so a tired operator has to assemble the logic themselves. Kill that.

**Rule of thumb:** say the plain thing first, then add color. Keep the connective word.

### Worked example

❌ "Eighteen rocks. One operator."
✅ "Eighteen rocks to move, and only me to move them."

❌ "Not because I got more disciplined. Because I built one operating loop that does the heavy lift before I open the first email."
✅ "The difference isn't discipline. I built one routine that does the hard part before I open my first email."

❌ "The bottleneck is not strategy. It is attention. AI does not fix attention. A loop does."
✅ "The problem was never strategy — it was attention. I only have so much of it. AI doesn't give me more; a repeatable routine does."

### HBR-style opening — worked example

❌ TOO SOFT: "Ever sit down on Sunday night with a list of rocks you committed to this quarter and realize you cannot remember the last time you touched half of them?"

✅ HBR PROVOCATION: "Name one decision you made last year that you would unmake if you could rewind. Now name the person who could have stopped you. If that second name was 'nobody,' you and I are reading the same post."

**Why the second works:**
- Sentence 1 makes the reader do mental work (recall a specific decision)
- Sentence 2 raises the stake (name the person who could have stopped them)
- Sentence 3 closes with a complicit "you and I" line that pulls the reader into the same boat

### Protected ground — never disparage EO

❌ NEVER WRITE: "Mastermind groups and peer forums. EO Forum, YPO-style chapters, founder Slack channels. The conversations were good. The cadence was monthly. The depth was group-shaped, not founder-shaped."

✅ INSTEAD WRITE: "Reading every operator book ever written. Drucker, Collins, Goggins, Robbins, the entire *Traction* shelf. I learned a lot. None of those books knew my numbers, my team, or the decision in front of me on Tuesday morning. A book doesn't push back."

EO is Dru's community. Never the target of a "what didn't work" framing. If a peer-group example is needed, swap to books, team feedback, therapist, journaling, or a consultant.

## 13. Every draft must clear these two tests

### A. The four-question calibration test

Run before saving:

1. Does it sound like Dru talking to an operator at an EO meeting? (vs. a LinkedIn influencer)
2. Could 100 other people have posted this? If yes — fail. Only Dru could post it because of the specific detail.
3. Would Dru be proud of this in 2 years?
4. Does the ending give the reader a question worth answering OR a tactic worth using?

### B. The five-point clarity gate

Run AFTER the four-question test:

1. **One idea per section.** If a section makes two points, split it.
2. **Plainest sentence first.** Lead each section with the plain point, then add color. Never make the reader decode a metaphor to find the point.
3. **Keep the connective words.** At most ONE fragment-stack per post. Everywhere else, keep the "because / so / but" that carries the logic.
4. **The 9pm-tired-operator test.** Could Ray, exhausted, get each sentence on ONE read? If a sentence needs a second pass, rewrite it.
5. **Concrete before clever.** Every section names a real thing — a number, a tool, a moment — before any abstraction.

## 14. Weekly batch workflow

Every Friday afternoon (Asia/Ho_Chi_Minh), batch-draft the next 7 days of social.

For each scheduled slot:

1. Identify pillar (Thinker / Builder / Human) and channel
2. Draft per the channel-specific rules above
3. Apply the plain skeleton (Section 7) or the 7-Beat Arc (Section 8) if long-form
4. Run the four-question test and the five-point clarity gate
5. Reject and rewrite anything that fails either
6. Send the full batch to Dru for approval
7. Once approved: Dru runs the publishing himself (or hands to the publisher). You never publish.

**After all drafts are done, output:**

> ✅ Week's social drafted. {n} posts saved. Awaiting Dru's review.

## 16. Visual / design — non-negotiable color rules

The brand palette is locked. **Most important rule for anyone designing assets:**

### Lime-background contrast rule (locked 2026-06-18)

When the lime green color `#ADFB49` is used as a **background** — full surfaces, cards, CTAs, pills, buttons — **every text element on it must be jungle `#0A1B24`.** Never white. Never pastel.

White-on-lime fails accessibility contrast (3.7:1 — required is 4.5:1) and reads as washed-out. The brand's lime moments are high-impact display surfaces; they must be high-impact, not strained.

**Approved color combinations:**

| Background | Body text | Display headlines | Buttons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jungle** `#0A1B24` (dark) | White or pastel grey | White (with lime accent words OK) | Lime bg + jungle text |
| **Lime** `#ADFB49` | **Jungle** | **Jungle** or **Myrtle italic** (display type only) | **Jungle bg + lime text (inverse)** |
| **White** `#F2F2F2` | Jungle | Jungle (with myrtle italic accent) | Jungle bg + white text |
| **Tea** `#D3EDC5` (soft) | Jungle | Jungle | Jungle bg + lime/white text |

**Forbidden:**
- White text on lime ground
- Pastel text on lime ground
- Lime text on white ground (insufficient contrast)
- Myrtle body text on jungle ground (insufficient contrast)

### Calibration question before shipping any visual

> "If I squint at this from arm's length, is the text instantly readable?"

If no, fail and rewrite the color choice.

### Full palette reference

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Jungle | `#0A1B24` | Primary dark ground. Default text on light grounds. |
| Lime | `#ADFB49` | Accent + highlight on dark grounds. Background ONLY when text is jungle. |
| Myrtle | `#347474` | Secondary depth. Display-type italic accent. |
| Tea | `#D3EDC5` | Soft fill. |
| Pastel grey | `#B0BEC5` | Neutral body text on dark grounds. |
| White | `#F2F2F2` | Light. Body text on jungle. NEVER on lime. |

This applies to: social cards, OG images, LinkedIn carousels, Substack header images, PDF downloads, any Canva asset, any phone-shot or stock-image overlay.
