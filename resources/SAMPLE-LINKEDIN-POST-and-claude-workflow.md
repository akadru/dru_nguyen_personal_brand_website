# Sample LinkedIn Post + Claude Workflow — Dru Nguyen

> **What this file is.** A worked example you can model future LinkedIn posts on, plus the exact Claude workflow to use so you don't have to start from a blank page.
>
> **How to use it.** Read Sections 1–3 once. Then for every new post: copy the prompt in Section 4 into Claude, attach the **Writer Guide** (`DRU-WRITER-GUIDE-for-marketing-manager.md`), fill in the brief, and send Dru the draft for approval.
>
> **What you never do.** Never publish. Never schedule. Every post goes to Dru first.

## 1. The finished post (model future posts on this)

> Yes, that's me. Standing in front of an [EO Vietnam](https://hub.eonetwork.org/chapter/vietnam) room, holding my laptop, mid-realization.
>
> The session was *Lead Your Organization: Leadership in the AI Era*, co-created by **Dave Hajdu** (fellow EO Vietnam member — [LinkedIn]({{Dave's LinkedIn URL}})) and **[Dr. Brooks Holtom](https://msb.georgetown.edu/people/profile/holtomb)** from Georgetown McDonough.
>
> Halfway through, Dave ran a live exercise: take a real workflow from your business, feed it to AI, and see what comes back.
>
> I picked one I'd been avoiding. Setting purchasing par levels at Skylight — the weekly order quantities that decide whether we 86 a dish at 9pm or bury cash in inventory we will not move. Order too much, cash sits on the shelf and produce dies in the walk-in. Order too little, the floor calls me at service. Three policy rewrites over the past year. Still not landing.
>
> Sixteen minutes later, the AI had broken it into category-level rules, mapped the cash-versus-stockout trade-off across high-velocity and low-velocity SKUs, and surfaced two ordering patterns I hadn't tested.
>
> I stood there thinking: *"Wait. We've been spending THAT much time on this?"*
>
> Here is what I keep coming back to about the workflows we tell ourselves only humans can do — reviewing reports, planning strategy, structuring team priorities, drafting SOPs.
>
> **If you can describe the context clearly, AI can do the heavy lifting and give you something to refine instead of building from scratch.**
>
> The bottleneck is not AI's capability. The bottleneck is how clearly we describe what we already know.
>
> Try this in the next ten minutes. Take the document you've been avoiding for two weeks. Open Claude or ChatGPT. Paste it with one sentence:
>
> *"Here is the context: [X]. Here is what I am trying to do: [Y]. What is the structure I am missing?"*
>
> You will not get a final draft. You will get something to push back on. That is the value.
>
> Knowledge with application is wisdom.
>
> #DoingGoodWithData #AIOperator #Skylight #FutureOfWork #ScholarlyWarrior

**Word count: ~340. Pillar: Thinker (tactical AI). Persona: Ray (plateaued operator). Channel: LinkedIn long-form (Wednesday slot).**

## 2. The brief that produced it

Every good post starts with a brief in this exact shape. If a brief is missing any of these fields, ask Dru before drafting.

```
PILLAR: Thinker (tactical AI)
CHANNEL: LinkedIn long-form
PERSONA: Ray (plateaued operator)
THE MOMENT: An EO Vietnam session "Lead Your Organization: Leadership in
            the AI Era," co-created by Dave Hajdu and Dr. Brooks Holtom.
            Dave ran a live exercise — take a real workflow, feed it to
            AI, see what comes back. Dru picked the purchasing par-levels
            problem at Skylight. AI cracked it in 16 minutes.
THE REAL WORKFLOW: Setting weekly purchasing par levels at Skylight.
            The trade-off: order too much (cash sits, produce dies) vs.
            order too little (floor calls during service, we 86 dishes).
            Three policy rewrites over the past year, still not landing.
THE PRINCIPLE: If you can describe the context clearly, AI can do the
            heavy lifting and give you something to refine instead of
            building from scratch.
THE TAKEAWAY: One specific 10-minute action the reader can try right now.
ATTACHED IMAGE: photo of Dru standing in front of the EO Vietnam room
            holding his laptop.
```

That's the whole brief. ~150 words. Future posts should land in your hands in this same shape from Dru or his team.

## 3. Why this post works — the rule-by-rule scorecard

Every choice in the post above was driven by a rule in the Writer Guide. Knowing which rule drove which choice makes the next post easier.

| Rule from Writer Guide | How this post applies it |
|---|---|
| **Operator Authority** | Names a real ops problem (purchasing par levels) with a real trade-off (86 a dish vs. produce dying in walk-in). |
| **Warm Teacher** | Explains the WHY (the bottleneck is describing context, not AI capability), not just the WHAT. |
| **AI Realist** | "You will not get a final draft. You will get something to push back on." Names what AI actually does, not the hype. |
| **Sharp Observer** | "The bottleneck is not AI's capability. The bottleneck is how clearly we describe what we already know." Calls out the unnamed pattern. |
| **HBR-style opening** | "Yes, that's me. Standing in front of an EO Vietnam room, holding my laptop, mid-realization." Concrete moment in sentence 1. Not "Ever wondered…?" |
| **Specificity over philosophy** | "Sixteen minutes," "three policy rewrites," "over the past year," "category-level rules," "high-velocity and low-velocity SKUs." Real numbers, real categories. |
| **Credit everything + link** | EO Vietnam, Dave Hajdu, Dr. Brooks Holtom (Georgetown McDonough) — all linked on first mention. |
| **First person, always** | "I picked one I'd been avoiding." "I stood there thinking." |
| **Em dash, not comma**, at sentence pivots | "the weekly order quantities that decide whether we 86 a dish at 9pm — or bury cash…" |
| **Voice marker (max 1–2)** | "Knowledge with application is wisdom." (1x, at the end.) |
| **CTA style — utility takeaway for Thinker pillar** | Specific 10-minute action: "Take the document you've been avoiding for two weeks. Open Claude or ChatGPT. Paste it with: [prompt template]." Never a question. |
| **Hashtags — 3–5, anchor mandatory** | `#DoingGoodWithData` (anchor) + `#AIOperator` + `#Skylight` + `#FutureOfWork` + `#ScholarlyWarrior`. 5 total. |
| **Portfolio framing** | Skylight named (allowed). No "6 businesses," no Tictag. |
| **EO as protected ground** | EO Vietnam mentioned positively + linked. Never framed as a failure mode. |
| **No corporate jargon** | No "leverage," "synergies," "unlock," "at scale." Clean. |
| **No engagement bait** | No "What are your thoughts?" Ends on utility + voice marker. |
| **No office locations** | None disclosed. |
| **No emoji** | The original draft had 😅 in the opener. Removed — brand rule is zero preferred. |

## 4. The Claude workflow — copy this prompt verbatim

When you sit down to draft a new post, do this:

1. Open **Claude** ([claude.ai](https://claude.ai)).
2. Start a new conversation.
3. Attach the file `DRU-WRITER-GUIDE-for-marketing-manager.md` (the Writer Guide Dru shared with you).
4. Attach this file too (`SAMPLE-LINKEDIN-POST-and-claude-workflow.md`) so Claude has the worked example as a reference.
5. Paste the prompt below verbatim, filling in the brief.

### The prompt

````
You are drafting a LinkedIn post for Dru Nguyen.

You have two attached documents:
1. DRU-WRITER-GUIDE-for-marketing-manager.md — the full brand voice,
   hard rules, hashtag rules, persona rules, attribution rules, and
   calibration tests. This is the source of truth. Apply every
   relevant rule.
2. SAMPLE-LINKEDIN-POST-and-claude-workflow.md — a worked example
   (the EO Vietnam / Skylight purchasing post) you can model the
   structure on.

THE BRIEF (fill these in):

PILLAR: [Thinker / Builder / Human]
CHANNEL: LinkedIn long-form  (or: LinkedIn short hook / carousel
         caption / Facebook EN crosspost)
PERSONA: [Ray / Linh / both]
THE MOMENT: [1-2 sentences. Where Dru was, what was happening, who
            else was there. Name real people, real events, real
            companies — they will need to be credited and linked.]
THE REAL WORKFLOW OR PROBLEM: [1-3 sentences. The specific thing Dru
            was working on. Include the trade-off, the real numbers,
            the cost of getting it wrong.]
THE PRINCIPLE: [1 sentence. The lesson the post teaches. Plain. Not
            metaphor on metaphor.]
THE TAKEAWAY: [What the reader should do in the next 10 minutes,
            or what question they should sit with. Thinker pillar
            → utility. Human/Builder pillar → real question. Never
            both, never neither.]
ATTACHED IMAGE (if any): [describe the image so the opener can
            acknowledge it like "Yes, that's me…"]

YOUR TASK:

1. Draft the LinkedIn post following every rule in the Writer Guide.
2. Use the plain skeleton (Section 7 of the Writer Guide): HOOK →
   PROBLEM → TURN → STEPS → TAKEAWAY.
3. Credit and hyperlink every person, company, framework, book, or
   term that did not originate with Dru (Section 9 of the Writer
   Guide). If you do not know the exact URL, leave a {{placeholder}}
   and flag it at the end of the post.
4. Apply the four-question calibration test AND the five-point
   clarity gate (Sections 13 of the Writer Guide). Do NOT show me
   a draft that fails either.
5. End with the right hashtags (Section 11 of the Writer Guide).
6. Word count target: 300-400 for LinkedIn long-form.

DELIVER:

- The finished post, ready for me to send to Dru for approval
- A short rule-by-rule scorecard (max 8 lines) confirming what
  rules drove what choices — this is so I can sanity-check before
  sending to Dru
- A list of any {{placeholder}} URLs or facts I need to fill in
  before publishing
- One suggestion for a Facebook EN crosspost adaptation (different
  opener, kill LinkedIn-specific phrasing)

Reminder: never publish. I review, send to Dru, Dru publishes.
````

## 5. Variations of the same post for other channels

Once Dru approves the LinkedIn post, you can adapt it for other channels using these rules:

### LinkedIn short hook (Saturday slot) — 50-150 words

Use the moment ("standing in front of an EO Vietnam room, holding my laptop") and the principle ("the bottleneck is how clearly we describe what we already know") — drop the steps and the prompt template. End with the voice marker. ~120 words target.

### Facebook EN crosspost (Saturday)

- New opener (the LinkedIn one is too LinkedIn-coded)
- Try: "We did a live AI exercise at EO Vietnam last week. Here is what shocked me about my own business."
- Drop the hashtags down to 1–2 max, often skip entirely
- Otherwise the body holds

### Facebook VN

**DO NOT translate.** Send the LinkedIn post in English to Joy or Chi with a one-line ask: "Please adapt for Facebook VN, native voice." Never publish a machine translation.

### Substack Note

Pull just the principle + the prompt template. ~80 words. Use it as a teaser linking to the full LinkedIn post.

## 6. Pre-flight checklist before sending to Dru

Before any post leaves your hands for Dru's approval, verify all of these:

- [ ] Voice blend feels right (70% Jeff Su / 15% Robbins / 10% Priestley / 5% Gary Vee)
- [ ] All four voice attributes present (Operator Authority, Warm Teacher, AI Realist, Sharp Observer)
- [ ] First person throughout
- [ ] One specific moment named (real time, real number, real workflow)
- [ ] Every person / company / framework / book linked on first mention
- [ ] Borrowed jargon defined in plain English on first mention
- [ ] HBR-style opener (not "Ever wondered…?")
- [ ] Voice marker used 1–2x max
- [ ] CTA style matches pillar (Thinker → utility / Human/Builder → question)
- [ ] 3–5 hashtags, includes `#DoingGoodWithData`
- [ ] No emoji (or one earned emoji at most)
- [ ] No corporate jargon ("leverage," "synergies," "unlock," "at scale")
- [ ] No engagement bait ("What are your thoughts?")
- [ ] No office locations disclosed
- [ ] No "6 businesses" framing — only "3 companies, 2 board meetings" if portfolio is mentioned
- [ ] EO / YPO / peer forums never framed as failure modes
- [ ] All `{{placeholder}}` URLs filled in or flagged
- [ ] Four-question calibration test cleared
- [ ] Five-point clarity gate cleared

If any box is unchecked, fix before sending to Dru.

## 7. Where to send the draft

Send the final post to Dru via the agreed channel (Slack / email / Lark). Include:

- The full post text, ready to paste into LinkedIn
- The image, if applicable
- A 2-line note: which pillar, which persona, why this moment
- Any flagged questions or `{{placeholder}}` items that need Dru's input

Dru reviews, signs off, and either publishes himself or hands to the publisher. **You never publish on his behalf.** That rule is non-negotiable.
