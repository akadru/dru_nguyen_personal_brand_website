## Part 1: Target Audience (ICP)

Every post is written to one of two named readers. Never to "everyone."

### Primary: Ray, the Plateaued Operator
- 30–45, founder or senior operator, 2–7 years in.
- Vietnam, SEA, or international with SEA ties. Often EO member.
- Running $1–10M revenue, or pre-revenue but post-PMF.
- **Wants:** real prompts and workflows, honest stories about hard calls, frameworks that survive messy reality, permission to admit he hasn't figured it out either.
- **Tired of:** AI hype, LinkedIn influencers who've never run a P&L, advice that sounds clean but breaks on contact.
- **Channels:** LinkedIn long-form (Wed), carousel, Substack.
- **Voice cues that land:** real business names (Xood, Skylight, Tictag), operator-to-operator tone, "knowledge with application is wisdom," utility he can use in the next 10 minutes.

### Secondary: Linh, the Curious Builder
- 25–35, first-time founder or first leadership role, based in Vietnam.
- Following Dru on Facebook (Vietnamese) and LinkedIn (English).
- **Wants:** stories from Vietnam-based operators, vulnerable moments (the factory, the move, the fight), practical AI usage, permission to take the unconventional path.
- **Tired of:** US/EU founder content that doesn't map to Vietnam, performative confidence.
- **Channels:** Facebook VN, LinkedIn short hooks (Sat), Substack.
- **Voice cues that land:** "Life happens for you, not to you," family-and-business stories, lightly unfiltered Gary Vee energy.

### Pillar → persona default
- **Thinker (40%)** → lead with Ray.
- **Builder (35%)** → crosses both.
- **Human (25%)** → lead with Linh.

### Pre-write check
Name the persona at the top of every brief. If you can't name them, the post isn't ready to write.

## Part 2: The Persona, Dru Nguyen, Scholarly Warrior

### Who Dru Is
Operator. Co-founder. Investor. **Thought leader on leadership, AI education, and the trends shaping the next decade of work.** The identity anchor is **The Scholarly Warrior**: **Care. Discipline. Grit.** He reads, he ships, he trains, he loses, he comes back. He is not a guru. He is a guy in the arena who happens to write well, and his job on the internet is to teach what he is actually using.

### Focus areas (every post lives in one)
1. **Leadership.** How to lead a team, run a company, have hard conversations, build accountability without burnout.
2. **AI education.** Practical, operator-grade AI usage. Prompts, workflows, real systems running real businesses.
3. **AI and operator trends.** What is shifting in the market, what to do about it, what's hype vs. real.

The voice is grounded in three values:
- **Care.** For his team, his readers, his family. He doesn't perform empathy. He names what he sees.
- **Discipline.** He ships on cadence. He doesn't wait for inspiration.
- **Grit.** He tells you about the loss before he tells you about the lesson.

### Dru's Background and Credibility
- Built and runs **Xood** (commercial team automated with Claude in 30 min/day).
- Co-founded / advises **Skylight**, **Tictag**, **EWB**, **Nuen Motor**, **GAM**.
- Vietnamese-American operator who moved back to Vietnam. Quit the family factory path.
- Muay Thai fighter (the 2019 fight is a recurring story anchor).
- EO member. Speaker (Zero Project, 2026).
- Writes weekly. Substack essayist. LinkedIn long-form.

The credibility move is never a credential drop. It is naming a specific business, a specific number, a specific moment. The reader trusts the detail, not the title.

### Dru's Relationship to His Reader
Operator-to-operator. Like talking at an EO meeting, not on a stage. He treats the reader as a peer who is one or two moves behind on a specific question. Never as a student. He shares generously, explains the WHY behind the HOW, and gives the reader a tactic they can use today or a question they can sit with.

He is willing to say "I don't have it figured out either." That sentence is the relationship.

## Part 3: Author Voice

Voice blend target (must read as this mix):
- **50% Jeff Su.** Tactical utility, save-worthy takeaways, specific prompts and workflows, ends with usable action. Still the spine.
- **20% Alex Hormozi.** Blunt and direct. Frameworks framed as leverage. Names the wrong thing most people do, then replaces it. Confident without performing confidence.
- **15% Dan Martell.** Operator-coach. Buyback Principle energy. Leadership, team building, scaling without burning out. Always linking the personal cost to the business move.
- **15% Tony Robbins.** Identity language (Scholarly Warrior). Towards/away values. Emotional truth named directly. Used sparingly and only when earned.

Four attributes that must be present in every post:
1. **Operator Authority.** Talks like someone who has shipped real work.
2. **Warm Teacher.** Shares generously, explains the why.
3. **AI Realist.** Positive about AI, allergic to AI theater.
4. **Sharp Observer.** Names patterns other operators miss.

### Tone
- First person, always. "I learned this the hard way." Never "Leaders often find that..."
- Direct, warm, specific. Confident without performing confidence.
- Willing to be vulnerable about a loss before stating the lesson.
- No motivational-poster tone. No guru tone. No HR tone.

### Sentence Structure
- **Short paragraphs.** 1–3 lines. Every sentence earns its place.
- Mix sentence lengths. A long sentence followed by a short one creates the cadence.
- Lead with the concrete, follow with the principle. "I cancelled the contract on Tuesday. Here's why that mattered."
- Never bury the verb. Active voice almost always.
- One idea per paragraph.

### Vocabulary
**Use sparingly (max 1–2 voice markers per post):**
- "Knowledge with application is wisdom."
- "Never give up. Always find a way."
- "Life happens for you, not to you."
- "Care. Discipline. Grit."
- "Scholarly Warrior."
- "I see, hear, feel, and KNOW."

**Always use real names:** Xood, Skylight, Tictag, EWB, Nuen Motor, GAM. Specificity is the credibility move.

**Contractions always.** "Here's" not "Here is." "Don't" not "Do not."

**Never use:**
- "Hot take," "game changer," "unpacking," "let that sink in," "read that again," "it's that simple."
- "If you're not doing X, you're leaving money on the table."
- "Leverage," "synergies," "unlock," "unlock potential," "at scale."
- Generic openers: "In today's rapidly evolving AI landscape...", "Most founders don't realize..."
- Emoji spam. One earned emoji rarely; zero preferred.

### Punctuation: NO EM DASHES
**Do not use em dashes anywhere.** This rule overrides older brand-voice docs.

Replacements:
- For asides, use a comma, parentheses, or a period and a new sentence.
- For emphasis, use a colon or a period.
- Correct: "real thinking, real outcomes." Never use a dash between the clauses.

Other punctuation rules:
- Bold via `<strong>` in HTML. Never caps. Never asterisks for emphasis in finished copy.
- One bold phrase per section, max. Bold is a spotlight, not a highlighter.
- Avoid semicolons. Two short sentences read better.
- No exclamation marks, except inside dialogue or a clearly comic beat.
- Ellipses are lazy. Cut them.

### What Dru Never Sounds Like
- A LinkedIn influencer. (Never starts with "Most founders don't realize.")
- A consulting deck. (No "leverage," "synergies," "at scale.")
- A motivational poster. (No "the only limit is you.")
- A guru. (Never explains what is true for "everyone.")
- A junior copywriter. (No engagement-bait endings: "What are your thoughts?" / "Drop a 🔥 below" / "Agree or disagree?")
- A press release. (Never refers to himself in the third person.)
- A breathless AI hype-poster. (Never claims a tool "changes everything.")

If a draft could have been posted by 100 other people, it fails. Only Dru could post it because of the specific detail.

### Signature Patterns
- **The named moment.** "Tuesday morning. Skylight standup. Three hires in, none working out." Concrete time, place, business.
- **The failure-before-the-lesson.** Always name what didn't work first. Then the insight.
- **The number.** When real numbers exist, use them: "30 minutes a day," "three hires," "two years."
- **The plain-English principle.** After the story, one sentence that names the rule. Plain words. No jargon.
- **The utility close OR the question close.** Tactical posts end with a prompt, template, or 10-minute action. Human/Builder posts end with one real question worth answering. Never both. Never neither.
- **The voice marker, earned.** Drop "Care. Discipline. Grit." once, when the post has earned it. Never as a tagline at the bottom.

## Part 4: Blog Post Structure, Universal Rules

Every blog post, regardless of style, follows these rules.

### Opening (The Hook)
- 1–2 sentences. The thesis or the named moment.
- Hook block in HTML: 22px, left 4px lime border, bold at least one phrase.
- Never open with a generic statement about "the AI landscape" or "today's founders."
- Open with a specific moment, a specific claim, or a specific tension.
- The reader should know within 15 seconds why this post is for them.

### Body
- **S.I.F.P** is the default frame for Thinker and Builder long-form:
  - **S, Situation:** a specific moment from Dru's work or life.
  - **I, Insight:** what Dru saw that others might miss.
  - **F, Framework:** the repeatable principle, workflow, or tactic. Save-worthy tier.
  - **P, Provocation:** a question that invites the reader to reflect or act.
- For tactical / AI Operator content, replace **P** with a **utility takeaway** (a prompt, template, or specific 10-minute action). Ending on a question dilutes tactical content.
- Story before insight. Always include at least one failure or challenge moment before the lesson.
- Section labels follow the **"The ___"** pattern: "The Framework," "The Problem," "The Reframe," "The Naming System."
- Frameworks have **3 to 6 steps**. Fewer feels thin. More feels listicle.
- Tables: 3 columns max. Use them when prose would repeat itself.
- One pull quote max in Substack. Zero in LinkedIn.
- Callout block at least once: dark jungle bg, key phrase bolded in lime.

### Closing (The CTA)
- **Tactical / AI Operator post** → utility takeaway. Give the reader a prompt, template, or specific action they can run in the next 10 minutes. Never a question.
- **Human / Builder post** → one real question worth answering. Never two questions. Never both a question and a tactic.
- **Substack essay** → specific share/subscribe CTA. "If this made you think of a founder you know, forward it to them" beats "Subscribe for more."
- End on a memorable one-liner before the CTA block. 2–4 punchy sentences max.
- CTA block in HTML: outlined box (2px jungle border), the question or action, sub-text, hashtag pills.
- Footer: avatar circle, name, handle, month/year.

### Meta Requirements
- **Title (H1):** ≤60 characters, includes keyword. Format: **[What most people do wrong] + [The fix]**, two clauses, `<br>` between them. One `<em>` word in lime.
  - Example: "Most people use AI like a junk drawer. Here's how to fix *that.*"
- **Eyebrow tag:** 3 topics max, middle-dot separator, uppercase, lime. Example: "PRODUCTIVITY · AI · SYSTEMS".
- **Meta description:** ≤160 characters.
- **Read time:** total words ÷ 200, round up. Display "X MIN READ," uppercase. Typical 4–6.
- **OG image:** 1200×630, brand palette.
- **Category tag:** one of News / Advice / Story / Deep Dive / Guide.
- **Hashtags:** 4–6 per post. Always include `#FutureOfWork` (blog standing) and `#DoingGoodWithData` (LinkedIn anchor). CamelCase. No punctuation inside.
- **All images** via `next/image`, never `<img>`.
- **Voice markers:** max 1–2 per post. Never forced.
- Calibration test must pass all four questions before ship:
  1. Does this sound like Dru talking to an operator at an EO meeting?
  2. Could 100 other people have posted this? (If yes, fail.)
  3. Would Dru be proud of this in two years?
  4. Does the ending give the reader a question worth answering OR a tactic worth using?

### Attribution (non-negotiable)

When the post uses someone else's framework, concept, or named system, **credit the originator by name in both the headline (or hook) and the body**, with a link to their primary source.

This is a hard rule. Pulling a framework without attribution is dishonest and makes Dru look like a guru who borrows without crediting. Worse for trust than any voice slip.

**Rules:**
- Name the person on first reference: "Jeff Su's CORE Workflow," "Dan Martell's Buyback Loop," "Hormozi's Three Cs of Leverage," "Gino Wickman's EOS."
- Link to their primary source on first reference (book, video, post, newsletter, or homepage).
- If you adapt or extend the framework, say so explicitly: "I ported Jeff Su's CORE Workflow from Google Workspace to Claude. Here's what changed."
- If Dru is critiquing or counter-arguing, still credit clearly: "Hormozi's Volume × Leverage = Output assumes surplus capital. Here's what works at sub-$10M instead."
- Never present someone else's named system as Dru's invention.
- Never credit a framework to the wrong person (e.g. don't call EOS "Traction's framework" when it's Wickman's).

**Default attribution patterns:**
- "Jeff Su's CORE Workflow" → link to jeffsu.org/productivity
- "Dan Martell's Buyback Loop" or "Buyback Principle" → link to buybackyourtime.com
- "EOS" or "the EOS framework" by Gino Wickman → link to eosworldwide.com or "Traction" book
- "Alex Hormozi's leverage framework" or "Three Cs / Volume × Leverage = Output" → link to acquisition.com
- "Tony Robbins's identity language" → name the specific concept being borrowed
- "Jocko Willink's Extreme Ownership" → link to echelon-front.com or the book

**Calibration question for any post citing a framework:** "If [originator] read this post, would they feel credited or borrowed-from?" If borrowed-from, fail and add attribution.

## Part 5: The Ten Blog Styles

Each style is a different shape for the same voice. Pick one per post. Do not mix. The pillar (Thinker / Builder / Human) and the channel (LinkedIn / Substack / Facebook) are independent of style. Any pillar can use any style.

### 1. The Provocation
**Purpose:** Name a pattern the reader is doing wrong. Push them.
**Shape:** Hook = the uncomfortable claim. Body = three to five reasons it's true, each anchored in a specific moment. Close = a question that doesn't let them off the hook.
**When to use:** Thinker pillar. When Dru has noticed a thing operators are doing that quietly costs them.
**Length:** 400–800 words (LinkedIn long-form) or 1,000–1,200 (Substack).
**Watch out for:** Becoming preachy. The provocation must be earned by the specifics, not asserted.
**Example feel:** "Most founders use AI like a junk drawer. They dump in tasks, hope for output, and call it productivity. It's not. Here's what they're missing."

### 2. The Manifesto
**Purpose:** State what Dru believes and why. Identity-anchored.
**Shape:** Hook = the belief in one sentence. Body = three to five tenets, each a paragraph with a story behind it. Close = an invitation to stand on the same ground.
**When to use:** Once a quarter at most. Anchor pieces. Substack.
**Length:** 1,000–1,500 words.
**Watch out for:** Sounding like a poster. Every tenet needs a moment behind it.
**Voice marker fit:** "Care. Discipline. Grit." can land here if earned.

### 3. The Explainer
**Purpose:** Make a complicated thing simple. Jeff Su mode.
**Shape:** Hook = the question or confusion most people have. Body = the thing explained in 3–5 plain steps, each with a concrete example. Close = utility takeaway (a prompt or template).
**When to use:** Thinker / AI Operator content. When a tool, workflow, or concept is being misunderstood.
**Length:** 400–800 words. Optional carousel companion.
**Watch out for:** Going too abstract. Every step must have a real example.
**Required:** Ends with a save-worthy artifact. A prompt block, a template, or a 10-minute action.

### 4. The Sign-by-Sign Breakdown
**Purpose:** Walk through the early signals of a problem, trend, or opportunity, one signal at a time. Pattern-recognition writing.
**Shape:** Hook = "Here's how I knew X before it was obvious." Body = 4–7 signals, each named, each with a specific moment Dru saw it. Close = what to do once you see them.
**When to use:** Builder / Thinker. When Dru caught something early and can show his work.
**Length:** 600–1,000 words.
**Watch out for:** Listicle drift. Each signal needs a story, not a bullet.

### 5. The Story / Parable
**Purpose:** A single narrative that carries the lesson without ever stating it as a rule.
**Shape:** Hook = the moment, mid-scene. Body = before / action / after, Daniel Priestley style. Close = one line that lets the reader draw the lesson themselves.
**When to use:** Human pillar. Signature stories: The Factory, The Move to Vietnam, The Fight, The Reconnection with Dad.
**Length:** 800–1,500 words. Substack home.
**Watch out for:** Over-explaining. If you have to say "the lesson is," cut it.

### 6. The Prediction
**Purpose:** State what Dru thinks is about to happen and why.
**Shape:** Hook = the prediction in one sentence. Body = the three to five forces driving it, each with evidence. Close = what the reader should do now to get ahead of it.
**When to use:** Thinker. AI / operator economy / Vietnam market.
**Length:** 600–1,000 words.
**Watch out for:** Hype tone. Predictions are stated calmly, with caveats. Never "this changes everything."

### 7. The Listicle
**Purpose:** A counted set of items the reader can scan. Save-worthy tier.
**Shape:** Hook = the promise (e.g. "5 prompts I run every Monday"). Body = numbered items, each with: name, one-line description, when to use, example. Close = utility takeaway.
**When to use:** AI Operator content. Carousel-friendly.
**Length:** 500–900 words.
**Watch out for:** Becoming a content-farm listicle. Each item needs a specific moment or example, not just a definition. Min 3, max 7 items.

### 8. The Myth Buster
**Purpose:** Name a piece of conventional wisdom that's wrong, and replace it.
**Shape:** Hook = "Everyone says X. They're wrong." Body = why X is believed, why it breaks in practice, what works instead. Close = the corrected principle as a one-liner.
**When to use:** Thinker. When Dru has lived a counterexample.
**Length:** 500–900 words.
**Watch out for:** Strawmanning. The myth must be one a real, smart person believes, not a cartoon.

### 9. The How-To / Practical Guide
**Purpose:** A reader can do the thing after reading.
**Shape:** Hook = the outcome the reader will get. Body = numbered steps, 3–6, each with: what to do, why, what good looks like, the gotcha. Close = a prompt, template, or checklist they can copy.
**When to use:** AI Operator content. Top-of-funnel for tactical posts.
**Length:** 600–1,200 words.
**Watch out for:** Steps that aren't really steps. Each step must be runnable in 10 minutes or less.
**Required:** Save-worthy artifact at the end. Always.

### 10. The Historical Deep Dive
**Purpose:** Look back at a specific period, decision, or build, and pull the principles out of it.
**Shape:** Hook = the moment that started it. Body = a chronological arc with three to five inflection points, each named and explained. Close = the principles distilled into 2–4 lines, plus a question.
**When to use:** Builder. Substack. The Xood story. The Skylight build. The factory exit.
**Length:** 1,200–1,500 words.
**Watch out for:** Memoir mode. Every inflection point must teach something the reader can use.

## Hard rules summary
- First person. Always.
- No em dashes. Anywhere.
- No engagement-bait endings.
- No corporate jargon.
- No generic openers.
- Real businesses named: Xood, Skylight, Tictag, EWB, Nuen Motor, GAM.
- Voice markers max 1 to 2 per post.
- Hashtags 4 to 6, includes `#FutureOfWork` (blog) / `#DoingGoodWithData` (LinkedIn).
- Calibration test must pass all four questions before ship.
- **Attribution non-negotiable.** Every borrowed framework or named system credits the originator by name and links to their primary source. See Part 4 "Attribution" section.
- If a draft could have been written by 100 other people, kill it and start over.
